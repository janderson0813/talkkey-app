TalkKey Privacy

Short version: TalkKey works entirely on your Mac. Your voice and your words never leave it. There are no accounts, no analytics, and no tracking.

What happens when you dictate

  • Your voice is recorded only while you hold the hotkey (or during hands-free mode), and only into memory. It's turned into text by a speech model running on your Mac and then discarded. TalkKey does not save recordings. (The only exception is a developer debugging setting, off by default and only switchable from Terminal, that saves recordings to your Mac's cache folder.)
  • Grammar cleanup (optional) uses Apple's on-device model (Apple Intelligence) on your Mac.
  • Typing the text: TalkKey briefly puts the text on your clipboard, presses ⌘V for you, and then puts back whatever was on your clipboard before.
  • Spacing: to decide whether to add a space, TalkKey reads the single character just before your cursor. It isn't stored.

What goes over the network

Only one thing: downloading the speech model (about 630 MB) the first time you use TalkKey, or when you pick a different model. It comes from Hugging Face (huggingface.co, Argmax's whisperkit-coreml repository). Like any download, Hugging Face sees the request (for example your IP address). Nothing about you or what you say is sent.

What's stored on your Mac

  • Your settings (hotkey, sounds, model choice).
  • The downloaded speech model, in ~/Library/Application Support/TalkKey.
  • A timing log at ~/Library/Logs/TalkKey.log: how long each dictation took to process, a pitch measurement, and the final punctuation mark. It never contains your words.

Permissions TalkKey asks for

  • Microphone: to hear you while the hotkey is held.
  • Accessibility: to notice your hotkey in any app, type the text for you, and check the character before your cursor.

Removing TalkKey

Quit TalkKey, then delete TalkKey from Applications, ~/Library/Application Support/TalkKey, and ~/Library/Logs/TalkKey.log.
